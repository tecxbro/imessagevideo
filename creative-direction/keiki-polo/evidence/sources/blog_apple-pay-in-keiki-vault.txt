Apple Pay in Keiki Vault: your agents get paid in Messages
A Keiki agent can now send a Pay card in iMessage that the customer pays with Apple Pay, without a link or a form. Here's how it works, who can use it and the one tool call behind it.
A customer texts a bakery for a birthday cake. The agent quotes it, and then the sale usually stalls: a payment link opens a browser, the browser wants a card number, and the customer puts the phone down.
Watch the demonstration
Now the agent sends a Pay card, and the customer pays with Apple Pay without leaving the thread.
 The card shows who's being paid, what for and the total. One double-click and a glance at Face ID, and it's paid.
How it works
The agent asks to be paid.
 It calls 
payments_request
 with the business, a description and the line items.
A Pay card lands in the thread.
 The bubble reads 
Pay $48.00
 with the description under it.
The customer taps it.
 Keiki Vault opens the card inside Messages: the business name, each line item, the total and an Apple Pay button.
Apple Pay takes over.
 Apple's own payment sheet opens. The customer confirms with the side button and Face ID.
The card settles in place.
 Once the payment is confirmed, the card shows 
Paid $48.00
 and the bubble in the thread is rewritten to 
Paid $48.00
. There's no second message to read.
Why this matters
No link, no form, no app switch.
 The card number never gets typed. Apple Pay already has it.
The money goes straight to the business.
 Payments run on 
Grosso
, and each business is paid through its own Stripe connected account. Keiki never holds the money.
Paid means paid.
 The card turns Paid only after Grosso confirms the payment, not when the customer taps. The agent is told never to say a payment went through until 
payments_request_status
 reports it paid.
The owner sets the limits.
 The agent can only request payments within a limit the business owner approved: the largest single payment and a daily total.
Who can use it
It's for businesses whose agent sells on iMessage: bakeries and restaurants taking orders, salons and clinics taking deposits, and services that send a quote and want it paid in the same chat.
To use it, you need:
The Payments ability
 turned on for the agent.
A Grosso account
 connected in 
Settings → Payments
, with the business added as a payee that's active and can take charges.
A dedicated Keiki iMessage line
 and a 1:1 conversation, as with every Keiki Vault card.
Keiki Vault on the customer's iPhone.
 They install it once from the App Store.
Pay cards are sent for physical goods and services. For digital goods, and on other channels, the agent sends Grosso's checkout link instead. On a phone without Apple Pay set up, the card offers 
Pay on checkout
, which opens the same hosted checkout.
How to get started
Connect your organization in 
Settings → Payments
. A new account starts in test mode, and cards show a 
Test mode
 badge until you switch to live.
Add the business under 
Payees
: its owner's email, the agent that collects for it and the payment limits. Grosso emails the owner to finish onboarding and approve the limits.
Turn on 
Payments
 in the agent's abilities.
Text the agent from an iPhone with Keiki Vault installed and order something.
For developers
There's no card to design and no checkout page to build. The Payments ability gives the agent its tools, and this is the call it makes:

Amounts are integer US cents. When Grosso marks the request 
native_apple_pay_eligible
 and the conversation is on a Keiki iMessage line, Keiki sends the Pay card and adds 
"pay_card": "sent"
 to the result, along with a note telling the agent not to send the checkout link too. Everywhere else, the result carries the 
checkout_url
 to send. Every call uses your organization's Grosso key on the server, so the key never reaches the model, the sandbox or tool output.
Refunds work the same way: 
payments_request_refund
 only asks, and the business owner approves it before any money moves.
