# Build one reusable iMessage filmmaking skill

Work in one Codex chat. Create a fresh private GitHub repository at `tecxbro/imessage-video-studio`, or continue there if it already exists. Build one installable skill that lets a fresh agent (1) recreate a complete reference film in a requested editor, (2) find and adapt a motion from a description such as “the photos slide underneath the name,” and (3) direct an original launch film from a product brief. A follow-up like “change the reply, hold the reaction longer, keep the sound in sync” must work through the same entry point. Ship the code, source projects and playable films.

## Give every worker the real inputs

Open [DATASETS.md](DATASETS.md) and [video-sources.csv](video-sources.csv) beside this prompt. The [original source list](https://github.com/tecxbro/imessagevideo/blob/c49482aec7b8ed0b840f97c61a5e5b860c4077b6/PROJECT-HANDOFF.md) gives the six original posts. Open all six MP4s in `originals/`. Extract the two ZIPs in `upstream/` for runnable code, 90 motion previews, event maps, example films and editable sound projects. Fetch the native simulator recordings, UI stills and the 99-film collection from their pinned sources in DATASETS.md. Check bytes and hashes. Give each worker the same resolved paths. If the user provides a separate source list from their Mac, merge by original URL or hash and preserve unique entries. Record any inaccessible or missing inputs. Never claim to have watched or heard material you could only index.

Clone and study all six repos at the exact commits in DATASETS.md. Read the runnable code, open the media and render through the normal entry points. Inspect the current heads of all seven codex-video-v3 branches and codex-main. Record the SHAs. Trace what the old branches actually used from the starting repos down to the file, function, asset or shot. Reuse working pieces under their original terms. Look beyond the starting repos, try competing approaches on the same shot, and keep the one that survives playback and editing. Record a short reason and a before/after for consequential choices.

## Run the seven roles in this chat

The team is Direction, Recreation, Motion, Messages UI, Dataset, Sound and an independent Observer. If the runtime allows only six children, take one builder role yourself and spawn the other six. Publish the roster, owners and dependencies to the team. Agents talk directly, exchange working code, rough cuts and stable event IDs, and run each other’s handoffs. Work in isolated branches or worktrees and integrate continuously in the new repo. The lead owns shared package metadata and the final build. Keep the user in this one chat.

- **Direction** takes a verified Keiki capability, studies ten credited makers and selects an original premise. Render competing cuts. Give a cold reviewer the film before the pitch. Ask what the product did, why the outcome mattered and where attention dropped. Recut that moment. The earlier train-chat demo was rejected. Explore a fresh premise. Product behavior and picture changes should carry the story.
- **Recreation** measures a whole film, including its ending, then rebuilds it with independent artwork, footage and audio. Support the requested editor and keep copy, timing, camera and layout editable. Compare every decoded frame on a common timebase and review the mix separately. Deliberately move a selection twelve frames late and prove the check catches it at the correct timestamp.
- **Motion** turns observed behavior into searchable, parameterized motions with rendered previews and event maps. Demonstrate Linq’s photos moving below a fixed name, the inverse motion, and native photo paging. Change copy, tempo, target and travel distance, then render the changes.
- **Messages UI** discovers the interface across the evidence and builds its states, layout and interactions. Follow real send, typing, scrolling, attachment, rich-card, carousel, Tapback, payment and authentication flows where the source supports them. Test changed copy and a reaction that stays attached while its message scrolls. Distinguish simulator, physical-device, provider and authored behavior.
- **Dataset** makes the media resolvable across machines, with creator, provenance, rights, dimensions, timing, audio presence and duplicates. Separate originals, authored assets, native captures, renders and diagnostic fallbacks. Identify gaps and maintain a repeatable weekly discovery process. Reserve genuinely unseen films for the Observer before builders touch them.
- **Sound** authors independently sourced, editable typing, send, tap, reaction, payment and transition cues as appropriate to the picture. Deliver stems, a cue sheet tied to event IDs and the final mix. Shift a picture event, deliberately leave one stale cue, show the check catches it, then correct it.
- **Observer** runs the baseline and new skill on the same reserved input in isolated fresh sessions, then gives a fresh director a different brief. Watch the full exports, listen, inspect cuts at native resolution and request fixes with timestamps. Distinguish automated measurements, agent review and actual human feedback. Mark anything untested.

## What earns completion

Exercise Python/Pillow + FFmpeg, JS/TS + GSAP, React/JSX + Remotion, HTML/CSS/SVG and native Motion Canvas through their real entry points. For each claimed route, show an editable source and a working render. Honor the editor the brief asks for. Keep reference films available for measurement and comparison. Use independent assets for recreations and final films.

Deliver one skill entry point, reproducible install and render commands, the media index, a full-length recreation, a finished original launch film, editable sound and picture projects, and one demonstrated follow-up edit. Have a fresh agent run the instructions from a clean checkout. Fix failures in the skill, re-render and let the Observer test again. Finish with links to the new repo, the playable exports and a short verdict with timestamps, measured results and remaining gaps.
