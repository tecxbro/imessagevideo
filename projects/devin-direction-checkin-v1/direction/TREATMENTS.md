# Check In — three moving treatments (authored choreography, not source-exact)

Promise (F1/F6): your friend learns you got home without either of you having to ask.
Format default: 1920x1080, 30 fps, ~24 s, UI sound first, quiet bed optional.

## A. "One thread, one walk" — continuous camera (CHOSEN)
One world, one camera, no cut to a card. Maya's thread with Sam: Sam asks "text me when you're home?".
Maya opens + > Check In, the card is configured (When I Arrive · Home · Walking) and sent; it lands in the thread.
Time passes *inside the picture*: camera pulls back, a walking route draws from the card toward a home pin,
the card's ETA rolls down, status-bar clock advances. Sam's phone enters on the same plane; Sam starts
typing "are you h" — the arrival lands first, card on both phones flips to arrived, Sam deletes the draft and sends a heart.
Motifs: conversation-handoff (compose-send study), selection-confirm, rolling amount (linq control study) for ETA, camera-focus.
Risk: two phones can read as a diagram; mitigated by one continuous camera move linking them.

## B. "Two phones, one card" — split stage
Sender left, receiver right, both full-height at once; the card is one actor crossing the gap and living in two
states. Very clear causality, but static symmetric layout is exactly a slide: two panels side by side for 20 s.
Rejected as primary: composition never changes, rhythm is ping-pong.

## C. "The question that never gets asked" — receiver-only
Entire film on Sam's phone. Cold open on Sam typing "are you home y…", a Check In arrival banner interrupts,
Sam backspaces. Then a single pull-back reveals Maya's earlier Check In card further up the thread.
Strong hook and one actor, but it hides the real user action (starting a Check In, F3), so the product is never demonstrated.
Rejected as primary; its payoff (deleted question) is borrowed as A's ending.

Strongest rejected alternative: B — clearer for a manual, flatter as a film.
