## Durable lessons for Sidehoe and the skill

1. Separate the handset, screen coordinates, native Messages controls, label/emoji paths, select/press state and camera. Catalog asset cues should preserve the actor's parent and actual lifetime. Compare cursor tip/handset registration and calendar material at native adjacent frames across the movement, including occluded intervals. A valid sampled hold and successful decode only explain the frames they cover. Linq's reconstructed overlapping apps and composer make this transferable.
2. Build true geometry and content before selecting a renderer for a suspected rendering fault. For a glyph or material mismatch, first compare authored actor values and evaluated shape to source. Compare lossless native renders at matched poses to isolate renderer differences. Finally verify encode color/sampling and final mux. Objective camera or matte tests should explicitly reject optically ambiguous neighborhoods, persist confidence and keep exact adjacent controls.
3. Encode text editability explicitly. Linq's traced paths and small colored emoji preserve source appearance within its historical scope. `native_components.py` reconstructs vector body/ink and isolates bounded genuine emoji. Several device views and app panels make a deliberate bounded-raster tradeoff. The boarding detail uses four measured screen materials at 4× authoring resolution with world poses derived from the phone/camera; a changed camera can invalidate those placements. Recreate reusable Messages geometry, fonts, proven glyph/emoji sources and stable edit actions independently. Historical crops teach ownership and masking. They do not authorize using borrowed UI materials where the current task requires independently authored artwork. [Linq component extraction](../../../../codex-direction-library/projects/05-linq-complete-v1/imessage-recreations/references/05-linq/native_components.py#L23) · [High-resolution bounded boarding](../../../../codex-direction-library/projects/05-linq-complete-v1/imessage-recreations/references/05-linq/steps-root-boarding-v18.md#L3)
4. Keep immutable input, archive identity and visual evidence in one chain. Linq separated scope, renderer parity, source matching and encode. A sparse review missed some high-impact failures, and a visual review path originally pointed at a live cache that later changed. Preserve the evidence bytes used for any verdict. When the scene changes under two independent patches, compare actor ownership and whole occupied frame intervals before combining. Per-file promotion and exception rollback protect the Linq cache well; whole-transaction recovery after a hard process interruption still depends on the journal. The final movie needs a new bound review after such a generation change.
