# Directing an iMessage film

## Select the task mode

- **Original launch film.** Start with the brief and verified behavior. Decide whose experience, observation or change the viewer will follow. An iMessage interaction can occupy a whole sequence, a single insert, or an offscreen beat if the audience can still trace what the product does.
- **Exact recreation.** The supplied movie determines composition and timing. Use the [reconstruction playbook](../references/reconstruction-playbook.md), its native frame mapping and aligned visual comparisons.
- **Component extraction or port.** Establish the editable boundaries, artwork dependencies and source-to-target tokens. Inspect the component contract and actual source.

For adaptation, study the source, then identify which of its techniques the new film needs. Catalogue search and `decisions.mjs` find shots and components. The choice belongs to the director.

## Establish the action before writing the edit

Identify who the film addresses, what the product gives them, the precise supported user input, the response they actually get and any limitation that changes the scene. Walk the product, inspect the owning code with a fixture or use primary evidence. For a fictional brief, label the fixture and exercise meaningful success and non-success states. Save the resulting copy, frames, transcript or trace the scene can refer to. Distinguish observed capability from an imagined setting, a compositing choice or a story consequence. A chat mock-up cannot establish an unsupported capability.

Think about the viewer's experience of that action. What could they be curious about during the first images or sounds? What will they recognize, discover or reinterpret by the end? A question, a contrast, a character's choice or a formal pattern can carry attention. Decide what each beat lets the audience know and whether it earns a cut or a hold. Opening, product reveal, runtime, typography, titles and ending follow the idea. A quiet hold that keeps a question open can work as well as a quick cut.

## Compare two moving treatments

Change one consequential editorial decision such as whose perspective opens, when the audience gets the result, what remains offscreen, or which image or sound is allowed to recur. Rough out the alternatives in video at roughly their intended pace and from the same verified facts. Give each one enough screen time to express the wager. Keep the strongest meaningful differences, even when that means changing structure, length or sound.

Study a relevant moving reference for a specific decision. Record the source, its version, the time or frame range inspected and what changes there. Look at the frames across the boundary, then inspect the same spot in the treatment. Describe the principle in terms of what the viewer can infer. Use a filmstrip or measured study for supporting evidence. Use the scene's own logic to set its timing.

On playback, find the cut, hold or repeated composition where the film begins to feel like a presentation. Identify the time interval, what attention was following just before it, what the frame provides instead, and what question, accumulation or consequence was lost. Check whether the product is taking a perceptible action or the edit is simply exchanging one proof panel for another. Test a concrete change in motion. This review can preserve a title or panel that gives the viewer time to read, reorients them, or changes the meaning of an image.

## Edit, screen and revise

- Keep the editable shot and cue plan with the project. Include exact copy, frame ranges, named actors and layers, local and camera motion, readable holds, evidence sources, sound anchors and what the viewer should be able to verify.
- Watch the encoded picture at intended speed and size. Inspect exact boundaries and reading holds where the eye gets lost. For exact recreation also inspect aligned native frames, separated error peaks and foreground differences.
- Share the actual rough picture and its pinned identity with sound. A useful cue names a visible action or an editorial change. Decide where to keep quiet, what sound can bridge a cut, and when a result needs room. Verify attacks, frame alignment, headroom and intelligibility on the muxed export.
- Give a fresh observer the movie before the brief, treatment or defense. Ask what they thought was happening, what they expected next, and which moment changed their attention. Use their observed understanding to revise picture or sound. Watch the revision with the final mix and record how it changed the intended beat.
- To test an instruction on a new brief, give a fresh agent the skill and the brief with the first project's creative decisions withheld. Compare perspective, order of discovery, use of Messages, and ending. If both pitches reproduce the same beats without support from their briefs, find and remove the wording that pushed them there.

## Deliver a reproducible edit

Include the earliest screened cut, alternate, accepted picture and mix, editable source and assets, verified product evidence and what changed after viewing. Record the tested commit, viewer links, render command and sound provenance. State whether verification used live product behavior, a fixture, or supporting documentation. A reconstruction package also retains native-frame mappings and comparison evidence. Keep these distinctions when recording scope or editability.
