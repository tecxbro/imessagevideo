# Direct an original film

Use this when the brief gives you a product and an audience. Treat the brief as
the source of the film's world. Open `studies/` only when the user requests
film studies. Keep finished examples out of a blind run.

## Decide what can happen

Identify who uses the product, who feels the result, and what they would be
able to do or notice afterward. If those are different people, decide whose
experience the viewer should follow. Give each important action to its actual
actor.

Look up unstable or consequential product details on primary sources. Make a
claim record with the exact on-screen or spoken assertion, a short exact source
excerpt, URL, verification date, and any conditions that affect the depiction.
Treat depicted causality, UI state, implied latency, and brand copy as claims.
Disclose dramatized data and modeled UI in the evidence. Drop unsupported
actions and absolutes. Decide whether the claim calls for a real execution,
a capture of a known behavior, or an authored demonstration of documented
behavior. State which you deliver.

## Find the film

Ask what a viewer can infer from a visible change. Work out what must already
be discernible for that change to mean something. An action can happen in a
single shot or across an edit. A film can begin at any point where the viewer
has something worth noticing.

When an important creative choice is open, make two meaningfully different
possibilities. Change the vantage point, dramatic situation, treatment of
time, or kind of evidence. Write a few lines for each about what the first
image lets the audience know and the consequence worth staying for. Pick the
one that can prove the claim through action in this production. If two still
compete, animate the passage where their difference matters and compare the
actual cuts. Decide on medium, frame, pace and sound from what the chosen idea
demands and what you can finish.

Keep only the text required for the viewer to understand or act. Allocate
frames to reading in motion at the intended display size. Preserve an object,
sound, gesture, or other recognizable detail when a jump in place, scale, or
time could obscure cause. Give a before-state time to register when a change
in the channel or interface is the proof. Consider if the ending can show that the world has
changed before it names the product or next action. Make the last image and
sound decisions on their own merits.

## Make the picture move

Build a playable proof passage early. Include enough of its surroundings to
test what it means. Mark who or what should have the viewer's attention and
where it moves next. Editable source needs to expose the timing and layers
that carry those decisions. Consult Motion when the intended behavior is an
existing motion and the UI adapter when the evidence uses iMessage.
Determine the required interaction and what remains fixed before selecting a
motion or renderer. Give invented motion meaningful parameters and start,
first visible change, and settle frames. Keep the picture clock authoritative
for event timing.

When the central v3 paths are present, use [the shared timeline contract](../contracts/README.md)
for events, state and cue anchors, and the [repo engine instructions](../RUNNING.md)
for a render entry point. Keep the film editable even if a specialty shot
requires an authored compositor or physical media. A standalone cut needs
its own render instructions, dependency list, FPS, frame count, and source
or provenance for its visual assets.

## Direct the edit with evidence

Play through if you have playback and capture contact sheets plus consecutive
frames straddling each important change. When evaluating through frame evidence,
identify that method. Trace what the viewer knows or can reasonably predict at
each edit. If it starts to feel like a presentation, find the first frame
where the viewer has learned all the current image offers and has no reason
to follow the next one. Check whether a prior cut, action, or attention cue
should change. Re-cut from the discovered point. Compare the weakest
decision against a real alternative and keep the stronger render.

Record one meaningful before-and-after with the cut names, frame numbers,
changed variable, and inference each version supports. Resolve further
problems by changing a specific decision and measuring its effect. Stop
cycling when another pass at the decision has no observed benefit and the
deliverable checks pass.

## Direct sound and finish

Give Sound the early picture, its hash, stable cue IDs with visible action
frames, and what the audience should hear or feel at the important passage.
Identify which sounds are diegetic and make a choice between treatments where
the passage depends on sound. Trade picture timing before locking. Re-resolve
sample offsets on the lock, hash the exact silent master, and send it with
the event map. Verify the returned mux against that picture and decode the
export. Check timing, peak headroom, delivery format, full decode and intended
end frame. Retain the mix and editable stems or treatment source. Say what
picture frames and sound measurements were checked and disclose any human
review still pending.

Deliver the finished film, editable project and rerender command, exact claim
record, and revision evidence. Where independent testing is requested, give
the reviewer the skill alone and keep your example out of their workspace.
Compare the resulting films after the test. If they share a premise, opening
or structure without a shared constraint that explains it, locate the wording
that taught imitation, remove it, and rerun the isolated check.
